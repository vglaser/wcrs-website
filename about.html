West Coast Research Symposium on Technology Entrepreneurship

An annual gathering of researchers of technology entrepreneurship, initiated in Seattle in 2003 and sponsored by six schools.

What we do

The West Coast Research Symposium was conceived as a unique arena where the latest research findings pertaining to technology entrepreneurship could be discussed in a collegial, highly charged, and well-informed setting. It serves as a forum for the presentation and dissemination of research focused on technology entrepreneurship. Participants include faculty and doctoral students from the sponsoring schools as well as others from West Coast universities and from the rest of the country and the world.

The intent is to improve existing research, stimulate novel research ideas, and foster new relationships and research collaborations. The symposium also addresses a significant barrier to research collaboration on the West Coast: the concentration of research and university education within a relatively small number of large universities that are physically distant from one another. It is an attempt to bridge those distances and bring together like-minded faculty and doctoral students each year to discuss and debate trends in technology entrepreneurship research.

Our genesis

Given the importance of entrepreneurship in the global economy, the dramatic increase in demand for entrepreneurship education, and the growing acceptance of entrepreneurship scholarship as an academic discipline, interest in technology entrepreneurship is strong among scholars based on the West Coast. A primary driver is the proximity of the key universities to major regional clusters of young, technology-based firms in Seattle, Portland, San Francisco and Silicon Valley, Los Angeles and Orange County, and San Diego. Yet technology entrepreneurship is a particularly challenging research area, because it requires knowledge of both the dynamics of technology and innovation and their interplay with launching and managing new firms.

Given this research challenge and growing interest, faculty from three universities initiated the West Coast Research Symposium in Seattle in 2003. Faculty from additional universities have since joined the consortium; the timeline below lists each school as it joined.

How WCRS works

A small conference run by its sponsoring schools

Rotating hosts

The symposium moves among the sponsoring schools. The host school runs registration and local arrangements for its year and handles the conference finances; external participants pay a registration fee.

One call a year

The steering committee approves a call for papers each spring, sometimes with a special emphasis for the year. Submissions are unpublished working papers; the committee assigns accepted papers to sessions.

Small by design

Around fifty to sixty scholars attend, most of them presenting. Sessions run with all discussion at the end, and faculty present even when a doctoral student is a co-author.

A consortium for students

A doctoral consortium precedes the main conference and gives students from the sponsoring schools mentoring and a first look at the field's current work.

Hosting a symposium

Each sponsoring school contributes to the symposium annually and takes a turn as host. The host provides the venue, the meals, and the local team; the steering committee sets the theme and the call; the University of Washington has handled submissions and papers in recent years. A school considering a future turn should speak with its steering committee member.

Papers

Conference papers

WCRS is a working-paper conference. Authors share drafts so that colleagues can read them before the sessions and improve them afterwards. For that reason the papers are shared with registered participants directly by the organizers each year, and they are not published on this site.

The public record of each symposium is its program: who presented what, where, and when. Programs for every year since 2003 are on the Conferences pages.
